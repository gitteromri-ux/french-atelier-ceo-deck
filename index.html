Bonjour,

Imagine a small café in a quiet neighborhood — not on a postcard, just on a regular Tuesday morning.

You step in. The barista glances up.— Bonjour.— Bonjour. Un café, s'il vous plaît.— Tout de suite.

Three short lines. The whole exchange takes less than a minute. You sit by the window with a small white cup, a sugar cube, the soft clink of someone else's spoon.

That's what French often looks like, day to day. Not a long performance. It's also the French we teach - a few clear words at the right moment, and a small space opens up — between you and a place, between you and a person.

Real Life France 2.0

Subject line: 3 simple French phrases for the boulangerie

It's just past eight. The bakery smells of warm butter and crust. The woman behind the counter looks up — and you only need three sentences.

"Bonjour, une baguette, s'il vous plaît." - Hello, one baguette, please.

"Et deux croissants." - And two croissants.

"Je peux payer par carte ?" Can I pay by card?

That's it. Three small phrases, and the moment carries itself. The baker nods. You step out with a paper bag still warm in your hand.
